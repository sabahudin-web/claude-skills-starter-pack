# Evaluation Rubric — Example for AI Judge Mode
# Target: acquisition-create-dm-variations (LinkedIn DM quality)

## Criteria

### 1. Voice Authenticity
**What it measures:** Does the DM sound like the author — a direct, plain-spoken consultant, not a corporate sales template?

| Score | Description | Example |
|-------|-------------|---------|
| 1 | Reads like a generic LinkedIn template. Corporate language, buzzwords, no personality. | "I'd love to connect and explore potential synergies between our organizations." |
| 2 | Mostly generic but has 1 human-sounding moment. Still feels like a copy-paste. | "Hey [Name], I came across your profile and was impressed by your work at [Company]." |
| 3 | Sounds like a real person but could be anyone. No distinctive voice. | "Hi Jana, saw your post on AI workflows — really hit home. Would love to swap notes." |
| 4 | Clearly a human wrote this. Specific, direct, has a slight edge or opinion. | "Jana — your post on async teams was surprisingly practical for a LinkedIn post. Building something similar for my clients. Curious what broke first?" |
| 5 | Unmistakably the author's voice. Direct, specific, a little contrarian, no filler. Sounds like something they'd actually send. | "Jana — saw you're running a 4-person team that replaced a 12-person one with AI. That's the exact thing my clients argue is impossible. What actually broke first?" |

### 2. Personalization Specificity
**What it measures:** How specifically does the DM reference something real about this person?

| Score | Description | Example |
|-------|-------------|---------|
| 1 | No personalization. Could be sent to anyone. | "I see you're in the consulting space..." |
| 2 | Generic category reference (role, industry). Not specific. | "As a founder in the SaaS space, you probably deal with..." |
| 3 | References one real thing (company name, a post topic). | "Saw you posted about async hiring last week..." |
| 4 | References something specific and shows they actually read it. | "Your post about hiring your first operator — the part about 'trust before systems' stuck with me." |
| 5 | References something specific AND draws a meaningful connection to the author's work or the lead's likely problem. | "Your post on replacing your ops manager with Notion automations — I've seen that go wrong in 3 out of 4 companies I've worked with. Curious if you hit the same wall." |

### 3. CTA Quality
**What it measures:** Is the call to action natural, low-pressure, and conversation-starting — not a meeting request?

| Score | Description | Example |
|-------|-------------|---------|
| 1 | Asks for a meeting, demo, or call directly. High pressure. | "Would you be open to a 30-minute call to explore this further?" |
| 2 | Implied meeting request or too salesy. | "I'd love to share how we've helped companies like yours..." |
| 3 | Asks a question but it's generic. | "What's your take on AI in your workflow?" |
| 4 | Asks a specific, relevant question that invites a natural reply. | "Did the async-first approach hold up once you had clients in 3 time zones?" |
| 5 | A question so targeted that not answering it would feel like leaving something unresolved. | "When you replaced the ops manager — was it the tooling or the processes that broke first?" |
